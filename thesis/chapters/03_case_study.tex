\chapter{Case Study: Office Building Scenario}
\label{sec:case_study_overview}
The case study models a single office building with solar panels installed in the rooftop, 15 electric vehicles charging at the building, a stationary battery storage system, and a grid connection with capacity limits. The simulation window is one day.

\subsection{Energy Consumption Data}\label{subsec:consumption_data}
\subsubsection{Data Source}
The energy consumption profile as stated in the previous chapter was derived from ELMAS dataset. A new timeseries was created with synthetic data.
\subsubsection{Profile Characteristics}

Figure \ref{fig:building_consumption} shows the hourly consumption pattern:
\begin{figure}[H]\centering\includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{figures/building_consumption_profile.png}\caption{Office building hourly energy consumption profile}\label{fig:building_consumption}\end{figure}
Annual statistics are a mean load of 6.73 kW, a peak load of 11.92 kW during business hours, an annual energy of 58,831 kWh, and a daily consumption of about 161 kWh (annual divided by 365).
\subsubsection{Validation}
The consumption profile comes from the ELMAS source data. The per-customer annual energy distribution, with mean 10,429 kWh and P75 = 58,831 kWh, reflects small to medium-sized office buildings. The 75th percentile is used to represent a medium-to-large office. The cluster shape has a clear working-hours peak and a low night baseline, which matches an office profile, and the added noise keeps the curve from looking overly smooth while preserving the annual total.

For this type of vehicle, a usage factor of 0.18 kWh/km was used. Lastly, a min SoC of 20\% will prevent extreme discharges and minimize battery aging.

\subsubsection{State Of Charge Requirements}
\label{subsec:mobility_soc_requirements}

The SOC when arriving typically ranges from 20 - 30\%, indicating that the batteries would have lost some charge over their morning trip of approximately 30-50 km. Similarly, the target SOC for departing is set at a level that should provide sufficient charge to complete both trips plus an additional reserve of roughly 100-150 km.

\subsubsection{Justification}

This schedule matches most people who go to work and then leave to go home. The longer parking duration allows the owner flexibility regarding where they perform their daily charging. Regardless of how often the driver charges during the day, all drivers need to arrive with a full battery that can last through their afternoon commute.

\label{sec:grid_parameters}

\subsection{Electricity Pricing}
\label{subsec:electricity_pricing}

\subsubsection{Realistic Wholesale Prices Used For Simulations}

The simulations used wholesale day-ahead electricity prices published by the European Power Exchange (EPEX SPOT), the primary wholesale electricity exchange in Europe, on March 12th, 2018. Real wholesale prices were downloaded from two sources: (i) The transparency platform run by ENTSO-E [ENTSO-E TRANSPARENCY], which publishes wholesale energy prices across all EU markets; and (ii) The Fraunhofer ISE Energy Charts API [ENERGY CHARTS] that also provide energy charts for individual countries. As seen in Fig. \ref{fig:price_profile}, the day ahead price profile resembles those typically observed in most of Western Europe with both peaks and troughs. On this particular day, there was a large trough during the early morning hours (between 01:00 -- 06:00 AM) when prices were very low. This occurred because it was night-time and thus overall electricity demand is very low while many thermal units are still producing electricity, especially since they are usually less expensive than gas fired units. However, as people begin to go to work, start their cars, etc., the demand increases rapidly, leading to steep increase in price until noon when the price begins to decrease again. At 18:00 -- 19:00 PM (6 -- 7 PM) prices reach their highest levels, primarily due to the fact that renewable resources have decreased significantly during the afternoon and evening periods as clouds become thicker and residential electrical usage for cooking and lighting becomes greater. \subsubsection{Price Characteristics}

As shown in Fig.\ \ref{fig:price_profile}, the wholesale price curve exhibits typical characteristics of wholesale pricing throughout the world; specifically, prices tend to follow a ``trough-to-peak'' structure where low prices occur during nighttime hours and higher prices occur during mid-morning to late-evening hours.

A common reason why prices drop at night is due to lower electricity demands caused by less commercial or household activity such as sleeping and/or reduced industrial activities; however, another important contributing factor is due to the types of generation used at night being generally cheaper than other times of the day (as thermal plants often produce electricity at night).

Another characteristic evident in Fig.\ \ref{fig:price_profile} is the rapid spike in price between mid-morning to late-afternoon hours as demand increases and renewable resource output drops.

Finally, another notable characteristic of Fig.\ \ref{fig:price_profile} is how quickly the price spikes up in the late afternoon hours as solar irradiance starts to decline (and thus solar output drops).
Justification: V2G uses more training episodes because the action space is larger (three actions instead of two). The discount factor is set to $\gamma = 1.0$ in V2G so later discharge opportunities are valued the same as near-term charging. The exploration rate $\epsilon$ is lower to emphasize exploitation once the policy stabilizes.

\section{Simulation Scenarios}\label{sec:scenarios}

\subsection{Scenario 1: Charging Only (V2G Not Permitted)}\label{subsec:scenario_charge_only}

\textit{\textbf{Configuration}}: multi\_ev\_config.yml.

In this scenario we investigate unidirectional charging only using 15 EVs that can only take charge. We use two algorithms (simple and RL (Q-learning)), an 80 kW Grid Capacity Limit and No Discharge Allowed.

\textit{\textbf{Objective}:} Establish Baseline Performance for Intelligent Charging without V2G.

\subsection{Scenario 2: Enabled for V2G}\label{subsec:scenario_v2g}

\textit{\textbf{Configuration}}: multi\_ev\_v2g\_config.yml.

This scenario enables bidirectional functionality (both charge and discharging) with the same 15 EVs as before. The simple and RL algorithms are able to perform both a charge and a discharge action. The sell-back price is given at 80–90\% of purchase price. Restrictions have also been put into place to prevent excessive discharging when preserving the desired state of charge.

\textit{\textbf{Objective}:} Evaluate the impact of enabling V2G technology.

\section{Metrics For Evaluation}
\label{sec:metrics}

Below are the metrics that will be used to evaluate performance of each solution.

\subsection{Economic Metrics}

Economic Metrics measure total daily cost (electricity purchased --- V2G revenue), cost/kWh, and savings relative to Level 1 baseline.

\subsection{Energy Metrics}

Energy Metrics measure total grid consumption, renewable utilization (percentage of charging coming from solar energy), and V2G energy discharged to the grid.

\subsection{Performance Metrics}

Performance Metrics measure target achievement, average final SOC at departure, number of hours over capacity limit (grid violations), and number of times below minimum SOC (SOC violations).

\subsection{Computational Metrics}

Computational Metrics report execution time/simulation, training time/RL methods, and how scalability affects performance as a function of fleet size.
