\chapter{Case Study: Office Building Scenario}
\label{ch:case_study}

This chapter describes the office building scenario, that is used to evaluate the smart charging algorithms. Data souces are provided along with parameter selection and justification for the chosen values.
\label{sec:case_study_overview}

The case study simulates a typical office building with a rooftop solar PV installation, EV charging infrastructure for 15 vehicles, a grid connection with capacity constraints, and a 24-hour simulation period.

\section{Building Energy Profile}
\label{sec:building_profile}

\subsection{Energy Consumption Data}
\label{subsec:consumption_data}

\subsubsection{Data Source}

The building energy consumption profile is derived from the ELMAS (European Load Model for Aggregated Sectors) dataset, specifically the Office cluster (\#5). The dataset file is \texttt{office\_single\_building\_timeseries.csv} and the source is the ELMAS file \texttt{Time\_series\_18\_clusters.csv} for Office cluster \#5. The building type is office/commercial, the target annual energy is 58,831~kWh (the 75th percentile of the per-customer distribution), and the temporal resolution is hourly with 8,736 data points for year 2018.

The profile was created by scaling the cluster load shape to a single-building target energy and adding stochastic variability (daily $\sigma_d = 0.08$, hourly $\sigma_h = 0.03$) to introduce realistic fluctuations, followed by re-normalization to preserve the annual energy total (see Section~\ref{subsec:building_model} for full methodology).

\subsubsection{Profile Characteristics}

Figure \ref{fig:building_consumption} shows the hourly consumption pattern:

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{figures/building_consumption_profile.png}
    \caption{Office building hourly energy consumption profile}
    \label{fig:building_consumption}
\end{figure}

Annual statistics are a mean load of 6.73~kW, a peak load of 11.92~kW during business hours, an annual energy of 58,831~kWh, and a daily consumption of about 161~kWh (annual divided by 365).

\subsubsection{Validation}

The consumption profile comes from the ELMAS source data. The per-customer annual energy distribution, with mean 10,429~kWh and P75 = 58,831~kWh, reflects small to medium-sized office buildings. The 75th percentile is used to represent a medium-to-large office. The cluster load provides realistic hourly patterns with a working-hours peak and an overnight baseline, and stochastic noise is added so the profile is not artificially smooth while preserving the correct total energy.

Solar irradiance data is obtained from PVGIS (Photovoltaic Geographical Information System)~\cite{pvgis}. The panel orientation is south-facing (azimuth 0°) with a tilt of 37° (site-optimum). The date is March 12, 2018 as a representative spring day. The temporal resolution is hourly and the data file is \texttt{irradiance\_pvgis.csv}.

\subsubsection{Solar Production Profile}

The hourly solar power production:

\begin{equation}
P_{solar}(t) = A_{panel} \cdot \eta_{panel} \cdot I(t) = 150 \, \text{m}^2 \cdot 0.20 \cdot I(t)
\end{equation}

Figure \ref{fig:solar_profile} illustrates the production profile:

\begin{figure}[H]

Peak production is about 14.6~kW at 15:00, with a peak irradiance of 487~W/m\textsuperscript{2}. Total daily production is 58.8~kWh. Solar production partially offsets building consumption during business hours (7:00--17:00), with a brief surplus at 15:00. The profile aligns with EV parking hours (6~AM--6~PM) and shows variable irradiance due to sporadic cloud cover typical of Paris in spring.

\subsubsection{Selection Justification}

March 12, 2018 was selected because it is a spring day with moderate solar production of about 59~kWh. It has realistic variable-cloud conditions typical of the Paris region, which provides a test of solar integration without unrealistically clear-sky irradiance. It also represents a typical seasonal pattern rather than extreme summer or winter conditions, and validated satellite-derived irradiance data are available from PVGIS (SARAH3).

Long parking durations provide flexibility for smart charging, and the SoC requirements ensure mobility needs are met.

\subsubsection{Price Profile Characteristics}

The day-ahead price profile for March~12, 2018 shows typical features of the French electricity market. There is an off-peak trough between 02:00 and 04:00, with prices of 9.56--12.00~EUR/MWh due to low demand and high nuclear baseload. A morning ramp follows between 05:00 and 08:00, when prices rise from 19.04 to 48.34~EUR/MWh as commercial and industrial loads come online. A daytime plateau from 08:00 to 15:00 keeps prices around 37--48~EUR/MWh, partly due to solar PV and hydro offsetting demand. The evening peak reaches the daily maximum of 62.00~EUR/MWh at hour~19 (6--7~PM) as solar production decreases and residential heating and lighting demand rises. After 20:00, prices gradually fall from 43.23 to 35.04~EUR/MWh as demand subsides and the nuclear fleet provides ample overnight capacity.

\subsubsection{Sell-Back Prices (V2G)}

For the V2G scenario, sell-back prices are set at 80\% of the day-ahead purchase price. This spread accounts for grid access and balancing fees, aggregator margin, and round-trip efficiency losses.

\subsubsection{Data Source}

The electricity price data was obtained from the ENTSO-E Transparency Platform~\cite{entsoe_transparency} and cross-validated against the Fraunhofer ISE Energy-Charts API~\cite{energy_charts}. The original market data is from EPEX SPOT SE, the power exchange operating the French day-ahead auction as part of the Single Day-Ahead Coupling (SDAC) mechanism~\cite{epex_spot}.

\subsection{Grid Capacity Constraints}
\label{subsec:grid_capacity}

\subsubsection{Capacity Limits}

The grid connection has hourly capacity limits:

\begin{equation}
P_{grid,max}(t) = 80 \, \kw \quad \forall t
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Justification}

The 80~kW limit represents a typical distribution transformer capacity for small commercial buildings. The building baseline consumption is about 8~kW, leaving roughly 70~kW available for EV charging, which is sufficient for 3--4 simultaneous Level~2 chargers. This creates a realistic constraint that requires intelligent coordination.

Without smart charging, 15 EVs at 22 kW each would require 330 kW, far exceeding the 80 kW limit. This constraint necessitates intelligent scheduling.

\section{Simulation Scenarios}
\label{sec:scenarios}

\subsection{Scenario 1: Charge-Only (No V2G)}
\label{subsec:scenario_charge_only}

\textbf{Configuration:} \texttt{multi\_ev\_config.yml}

This scenario evaluates unidirectional charging with 15 EVs that have charge-only capability, the Simple and RL (Q-Learning) algorithms, an 80~kW grid capacity limit, and no discharge allowed.

\textbf{Purpose:} Establish baseline performance for intelligent charging without V2G.

\subsection{Scenario 2: V2G-Enabled}
\label{subsec:scenario_v2g}

\textbf{Configuration:} \texttt{multi\_ev\_v2g\_config.yml}

This scenario adds bidirectional capability with the same 15 EVs, the Simple and RL algorithms (with a discharge action), sell-back prices set at 80--90\% of purchase prices, and discharge constraints that preserve the desired SoC.

\textbf{Purpose:} Quantify additional benefits of V2G technology.

\section{Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

The following metrics are used to evaluate each approach.

\subsection{Economic Metrics}

Economic metrics include total daily cost, defined as electricity purchases minus V2G revenue (€); cost per kWh, the average cost per kWh charged (€/kWh); and savings versus baseline, the percentage cost reduction compared to Level~0.

\subsection{Energy Metrics}

Energy metrics include total grid consumption, the energy purchased from the grid (kWh); renewable utilization, the percentage of charging supplied by solar (\%); and V2G energy, the total energy discharged back to the grid (kWh, V2G only).

\subsection{Performance Metrics}

Performance metrics include target achievement, the percentage of EVs reaching the desired SoC (\%); average final SoC at departure time across the fleet (\%); grid violations measured as hours exceeding grid capacity; and SoC violations as instances below the minimum SoC.

\subsection{Computational Metrics}

Computational metrics include execution time per simulation (seconds), training time for learning-based methods (seconds), and scalability as performance with varying fleet sizes.

\subsection{Code Structure}

The implementation follows object-oriented design. The core models are Building for energy consumption, solar production, and battery behavior; ElectricVehicle for battery dynamics and charging or discharging; and Grid for pricing and capacity constraints. The charging systems are MultiEVChargingSystem for charge-only algorithms and MultiEVV2GChargingSystem for V2G-enabled algorithms. Simulation orchestration is handled by \texttt{multi\_ev\_simulator} for charge-only scenarios and \texttt{multi\_ev\_v2g\_simulator} for V2G scenarios.
