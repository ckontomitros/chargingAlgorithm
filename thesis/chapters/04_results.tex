\chapter{Results}
\label{ch:results}

In this chapter we present simulation results for both charge-only and V2G scenarios. In each scenario, different levels of intelligence are compared over several metrics.

\section{Summary of Results}
\label{sec:results_summary}

Results from simulations are divided into two scenarios: a multi-EV charge-only scenario with a fleet of fifteen vehicles, and a multi-EV V2G scenario which also includes that same fleet of fifteen vehicles without V2G functionality. Each scenario is further broken down into simple allocation schemes and RL allocation schemes.

At a wholesale price we're not recovering anything if we try to discharge (assuming an 80\% sell-back rate). Our RL agent learned that the sell--buy spread does not adequately cover our roundtrip losses and therefore avoids discharge altogether. This illustrates the more general point: how to make V2g work? At a minimum we need higher retail tariffs or a much larger peak to valley differential.

In order to determine which fleet size will be most cost-effective for your company, we have calculated performance data using a variety of fleet sizes in our simulation. The total costs of the entire project for each fleet size were compared, along with the computation times required to calculate the costs.

To illustrate this point, Table \ref{tab:fleet_scaling} shows the cost per vehicle for various sized fleets. These calculations demonstrate how the cost per vehicle decreases as the fleet size increases, illustrating the concept of economies of scale. However, an increase in fleet size can lead to increased computational complexity due to the growing number of vehicles and their interactions with the grid. This causes a corresponding increase in computation time. As seen in Table \ref{tab:fleet_scaling}, the simple model has identical costs at all three fleet sizes (because it does not take into account the actual demand of the building or the solar production), while the reinforcement learning model provides better results than the simple model but exhibits higher computation times.

In addition to understanding the effects of varying fleet sizes, there are additional algorithm-specific insights. These include understanding how a particular algorithm (i.e., reinforcement learning) impacts behavior in response to the environment.

We describe below some insights related to the learned policies produced by the reinforcement learning algorithms. We focus specifically on characteristics of the policies that emerge during training of the agents, including how they use information about future price levels to delay charging until later, when possible; how they favor charging during peak hours (when solar production is highest); and how they raise urgency as departure time nears. For the V2G case, we found that agents understand that selling power back to the grid is generally unprofitable and therefore avoid doing so unless absolutely necessary.
